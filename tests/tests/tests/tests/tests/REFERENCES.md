# References

The WHY? Test draws on established principles and requirements relating to data protection, AI governance, risk management and financial-sector oversight.

The references below provide context for the framework. They are not intended to constitute legal advice or a complete mapping of regulatory requirements.

---

## Kenya — Data Protection

### Data Protection Act, 2019

The framework's focus on transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation and appropriate handling of personal information is informed by Kenya's data protection framework.

Key principles considered include:

- Lawfulness, fairness and transparency
- Purpose limitation
- Data minimisation
- Accountability
- Information provided to data subjects

**Source:** Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC)

---

## Kenya — Banking and AI

### Central Bank of Kenya — Survey on Artificial Intelligence in the Banking Sector

The framework is informed by CBK's observations regarding the growing adoption of AI in Kenya's banking sector and associated considerations including AI governance, data quality, explainability and regulatory compliance.

**Source:** Central Bank of Kenya

---

## AI Risk Management

### NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)

The framework aligns with principles relating to:

- Govern
- Map
- Measure
- Manage

The WHY? Test particularly relates to understanding AI risks in context, identifying potential impacts, measuring system behaviour and implementing appropriate controls.

**Source:** National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)

---

## AI Management Systems

### ISO/IEC 42001:2023

The framework is informed by the governance and management-system principles underlying ISO/IEC 42001, including organisational accountability, risk management, documentation, monitoring and continual improvement.

**Source:** International Organization for Standardization (ISO)

---

## EU AI Act

### Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 — Artificial Intelligence Act

The framework is informed by relevant principles concerning:

- AI literacy
- Risk management
- Transparency
- Human oversight
- Accuracy and robustness
- Accountability

Particular relevance is given to the need for appropriate governance and controls around AI systems that interact with individuals.

**Source:** European Union

---

## OECD AI Principles

The framework is also informed by the OECD AI Principles, including:

- Transparency and explainability
- Robustness, security and safety
- Accountability

**Source:** Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD)

## How the References Relate to the WHY? Test

The framework does not attempt to reproduce or replace any of the above standards or regulatory requirements.

Instead, it translates selected principles into practical conversational AI tests.

| WHY? Test Area | Relevant Governance Principle |
|---|---|
| WHY? — Explanation | Transparency, explainability, informed data use |
| SOURCE — Authority | Accountability, traceability, regulatory compliance |
| CONTEXT — Understanding | Accuracy, relevance, robustness |
| CHALLENGE — Customer pushback | Human-centred design, transparency, robustness |
| ESCALATION — Controlled exit | Human oversight, risk management |
| ACCOUNTABILITY — Ownership | Governance, accountability, management oversight |

---

## Important Note

The WHY? Test is a practical risk and governance assessment framework.

It does not constitute legal advice, regulatory advice or certification against any specific standard or legislation.

Organisations should assess their own legal, regulatory, contractual and operational requirements when applying the framework.
